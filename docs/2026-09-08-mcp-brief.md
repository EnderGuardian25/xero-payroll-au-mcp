# Build brief — xero-payroll-au-mcp

**Date:** 2026-09-08 · **Status:** Starting point for the build
**API authority:** `2026-07-21-xero-integration.md` — verified against the OpenAPI spec

---

## 1. Why this exists, in one paragraph

The official Xero MCP server's payroll tools are restricted to NZ and UK, and cover employees, leave
and timesheets only. There is no path in it to a pay run, a payslip, a superannuation line, a super
fund, a membership or STP. Australia's Payday Super regime — in force since 1 July 2026 — makes
per-employee superannuation data the thing every compliance tool needs to read, so this fork adds
the Payroll AU read surface that gap leaves open.

## 2. Scope

### In

- Roughly 35 read-only Payroll AU tools (§4 below)
- The Accounting API reads that let a consumer see super money leaving: `BankTransactions` filtered
  to the Superannuation Payable liability account
- Bearer-token auth so one process can serve many tenants without holding credentials
- Two build-failing guards over the whole surface: read-only, and no TFN/BSB/account number
- Recorded response fixtures per tool, captured from a real AU organisation

### Out

- **Writes of any kind**, permanently — including the write tools inherited from the fork, which are
  stripped rather than left unregistered
- **Compliance math.** No deadline calculation, no shortfall, no penalty estimation. This server
  reports what Xero says; interpreting it is a consumer's job
- **STP filing status and fund-level receipt confirmation**, because Xero exposes neither (§6)
- Storing per-organisation credentials at rest

## 3. The one decision that shapes everything else

**Hold no credentials.** Run in bearer-token mode: the caller performs the OAuth exchange and passes
a token per call. The server becomes stateless and therefore shareable.

The alternative — Custom Connections mode with `client_credentials` in the process environment —
binds one process to one Xero organisation, because the credentials are environment variables. That
is the right shape for one developer's Claude Desktop and the wrong shape for anything serving a
book of client organisations.

A caller-supplied token also means the server never becomes the most attractive target in the
system. A store of fifty organisations' `client_secret` values would be.

## 4. Tool surface

Grouped by what a consumer is trying to answer. Every tool returns a hand-defined shape.

**Pay runs — "what was paid, and when"**
`list-payroll-payruns` (with `If-Modified-Since`, `where`, `order`, `page`) ·
`get-payroll-payrun`

Carries `PayRunStatus` (DRAFT/POSTED), `PaymentDate`, period start and end, totals including
`Super`, and `UpdatedDateUTC`. Detail includes `Payslips[]`.

**Payslips — "how much super, for whom, to which fund"**
`get-payroll-payslip` · `list-payroll-payslips-for-payrun`

**`SuperannuationLines[]` is the reason this repo exists.** Each line carries `SuperMembershipID`,
`ContributionType` (SGC / SALARYSACRIFICE / …), `CalculationType` and `Amount`. Expose
`ContributionType` — a consumer computing superannuation guarantee obligations must be able to tell
an SGC line from salary sacrifice.

**Employees and funds — "where was it supposed to go"**
`list-payroll-employees` · `get-payroll-employee` (`SuperMemberships[]`) ·
`list-payroll-superfunds` · `list-payroll-superfund-products`

`get-payroll-employee` is where the PII risk concentrates. The live payload carries the tax file
number and bank account details. Define the return shape by hand, field by field.

**Settings and rates — "how this organisation is configured"**
`get-payroll-settings` · `list-payroll-payitems` · `list-payroll-calendars` ·
`list-payroll-earnings-rates` · `list-payroll-leave-types`

**Surface the Payday Super fields:** `IsQualifyingEarnings` on earnings rates, pay items and leave
types, and `IncludeLeaveLoadingInQualifyingEarnings` on the employee tax declaration. Xero has
enforced validation on these since 1 September 2026. A consumer cannot compute qualifying earnings
without them.

**Timesheets and leave** — inherited from the fork; keep the reads, strip the writes.

**Accounting — "did the money leave"**
`list-bank-transactions` scoped to the Superannuation Payable account.

Note honestly what this can and cannot establish: it shows money leaving the employer, not arriving
at the fund. Tool descriptions should say so, because the legal test under Payday Super is receipt
by the fund and a consumer that conflates the two will produce a wrong answer.

## 5. The two guards

Both run over the **entire registered tool surface**, in CI, and fail the build.

**Read-only.** Enumerate every registered tool and assert none can mutate. The fork ships write
tools — invoice creation, contact updates, payment recording, timesheet mutation — so this starts as
a removal job and stays as a regression test. A future contributor adding a convenient write tool
must be stopped by CI, not by review.

**No TFN, BSB, or bank account number.** Enumerate every tool's recorded fixture and assert none
contains a value matching those shapes, plus assert no tool returns a raw Xero response object.
Hand-defined shapes are the mechanism; the test is what keeps them honest as the surface grows.

The reasoning worth writing into the test file: a 35-tool surface will eventually gain a tool nobody
reviewed closely, and that is precisely the one that leaks.

## 6. Three gaps that are not solvable here

Do not spend time hunting for a way around these. All three are verified.

1. **STP filing status has no API.** No submission endpoints, no timestamps. Nearest proxy: pay run
   `POSTED` plus `UpdatedDateUTC`.
2. **Auto Super batch status is UI-only.** Filed → Sent → Completed happens via Beam and is not
   exposed. The uservoice request has been open since August 2023. **Nothing this server can offer
   confirms a contribution reached a fund.** That needs a clearing-house API; Beam is the best
   documented.
3. **No payroll webhooks.** Accounting resources only. So expose `If-Modified-Since` on every list
   tool and let consumers poll incrementally.

## 7. Granular scopes — read before choosing scopes

Connections created on or after **29 April 2026** use granular scopes, and **`accounting.journals.read`
is not among them.** `accounting.transactions` split into `accounting.invoices`,
`accounting.payments`, `accounting.banktransactions`, `accounting.manualjournals`.

Every connection created for this server falls after that date, so `GET /Journals` is unavailable —
plan the Superannuation Payable reads around `BankTransactions`.

Payroll scopes needed: `payroll.payruns.read`, `payroll.payslip.read`, `payroll.employees.read`,
`payroll.settings.read`. Plus `accounting.settings.read` and `accounting.banktransactions` for the
liability-account reads. **Whoever authorises a Custom Connection must hold Payroll Admin** in that
organisation.

## 8. Rate limits, and designing for them

60/minute/tenant · 5,000/day/tenant · 5 concurrent/tenant · 10,000/minute app-wide. `Retry-After` on
429.

5,000 a day per tenant sounds generous until a consumer backfills a year of pay runs across fifty
organisations. Make that hard to do by accident: honour `Retry-After`, cap concurrency per tenant at
5, and consider surfacing remaining budget so a consumer can pace itself.

## 9. Suggested build order

Each step is independently verifiable, and the riskiest unknown comes early.

| # | Step | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Fork, strip every write tool, stand up both guards in CI | Do the removal before adding 35 tools, so the guards are proven on a small surface and every later tool is born under them |
| 2 | Bearer-token auth, tenant passed per call, plus local test scaffolding that can mint a token | Without this nothing downstream is testable |
| 3 | Pay runs and payslips, including `SuperannuationLines[]` | The core value. Nothing else in the repo matters if this is wrong |
| 4 | Employees, memberships, super funds | Highest PII risk, so build it once the guards are known to work |
| 5 | Settings, pay items, calendars, earnings rates, and the Payday Super fields | Needed to interpret the data from steps 3 and 4 |
| 6 | `BankTransactions` on the Superannuation Payable account | **Verify against a real AU org with Auto Super early** — whether a super payment is even visible as a BankTransaction is unconfirmed, and a consumer's whole receipt story depends on the answer |
| 7 | HTTP transport, if remote callers are wanted | The official server is stdio-only. Not needed for a Claude Desktop consumer |
